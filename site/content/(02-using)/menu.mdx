---
title: The menu bar
description: Start and stop the webcam, read the phone's status and pick the picture, all from the menu bar.
---

Everything starts from the camera icon in the menu bar.

- **Start Webcam** and **Stop Webcam** sit at the top of the menu once a phone is ready. Until then, that spot says **Finish Setup…** and the next step.
- **The header** shows the phone's name next to its status, how it's connected (USB or Wi-Fi), its temperature and battery, and the live picture. A warm phone or a low battery changes color, and you can hover over it to see why. With no phone on USB or Wi-Fi, the picture is a gray screen and the readings are dashes.
- **Phone, Capture Mode, Orientation and Camera** each show what's picked. Start, Stop and the choices in the submenus leave the menu open, so you see the picture come up or the check mark move. Click the icon again, or anywhere else, to close it.
- **The menu bar icon** turns red with the call's clock while you're live.
- **Show Preview** opens a picture-only window, separate from what call apps see. Its size never changes what call apps get.

[Capture mode and orientation](/picture) covers the picture choices.

## If the cable comes loose

If the cable comes loose mid-call, the call keeps its camera and the picture comes back by itself when you plug the phone back in. If the phone runs hot, a notification offers to switch to 1080p at 30 fps.

## Why call apps say "OBS Virtual Camera"

While you're live, the menu says which camera to pick, and its **Why is it called that?** link explains this too.

The app sends its picture through OBS's camera, and OBS decides the name that call apps show. A camera of the app's own, named after your phone, needs a camera extension signed with a paid Apple Developer ID, which this project doesn't have yet.
